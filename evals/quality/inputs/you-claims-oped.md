# The Morning I Knew We Were Done

You know the feeling. You've built something for years, and then some random Tuesday you realize nobody needs it anymore. We've all been there. You show up, you care about the thing, you've given it your best years, and one morning you can just feel that the ground has shifted under you.

For me it was a Tuesday in November. You wake up, check the dashboard, and you feel that particular chill. You know exactly what I mean: the number down 40%, your chest tightening before coffee.

Here's what nobody tells you. When your product dies, you go through the five stages in about a week. You tell yourself it'll rebound. You bargain with the metrics. Then one morning you're at your kitchen table with a spreadsheet and you understand that the market told you a fact.

I built this thing with my own hands. You get attached, that's just human. You defend the roadmap. You refresh analytics at 3 a.m. until you finally have to admit what everyone around you already knows.

We all fear irrelevance. We all spend our thirties terrified that the world will move on without us and nobody will send a courtesy email. That Tuesday I learned that fear and reality aren't in cahoots: the world had moved on two years earlier and I'd been too busy shipping to notice.

So if you're watching a number fall and telling yourself it's seasonality: it probably isn't. You already know what you know. Trust the spreadsheet, not the story.

And when it's gone, you'll find out what every founder eventually learns. It was never the product that made you valuable. You'll sit in your kitchen, the sun will come up anyway, and you will build the next thing with hands that already know one way not to do it.
